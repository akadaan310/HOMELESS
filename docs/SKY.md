# Observed sky and constructed patterns

`worldloom.sky` keeps the measured catalog separate from game overlays. The source is HYG 4.1, 119,625 stellar rows after excluding Sol. The downloaded release at commit `c7f7f883fe678cc7680169a50ccd7dcc49b060ce` is byte-identical to the provided REALMS input. Its SHA-256 is `d9f69fd86bbf90a4e4d52b4c5c53eacfa6dfc0bfdef85bfd94f095e0bebe4ebd`. The source CSV is retained as deterministic gzip; immutable numeric arrays are a derived index. HYG is David Nash / Astronexus' compilation from Hipparcos, Yale Bright Star and Gliese catalogs, licensed CC BY-SA 4.0. Source URL, byte count, exact revision, download timestamp, upstream equivalence and derivative hash are in `data/sky/source.json`; the supplied license and README are beside it.

Call `overview()` for source metadata and quality masks. `observer(lat, lon, at, epoch=None, max_mag=6.5, limit=100, height_m=0)` returns JSON data with source IDs, original J2000 positions, epoch-adjusted positions, geometric altitude/azimuth, observer coordinates, horizon and gaps. The default deterministic fixture time is 2026-10-20 02:00 UTC. Time strings require a UTC offset. Returned stars are sorted by magnitude and bounded to 2,000. Only one full catalog is cached.

Positions use unit vectors, angular separations use `atan2(cross,dot)`, and proper motions become a pole-safe tangent vector. Constant angular-speed great-circle transport carries both position and tangent, so forward/backward transport is invertible. IAU 1976 precession rotates into the mean equinox of the observation date. IAU 1982 GMST approximates UT1 with UTC for geometric observer coordinates. Epochs outside 1900–2100 are refused. This is a game-scale approximation: source error covariances, annual aberration, nutation, perspective acceleration, topocentric stellar parallax, terrain occlusion and atmospheric refraction are absent. Weather remains unknown. A star above the geometric horizon is not a claim that it is visible through daylight or clouds. Positive observer height lowers the ideal spherical horizon; negative height does not invent underground visibility.

`body_reference('Sun'|'Moon', at)` replays pinned, source-hashed JPL Horizons DE441 geocentric ICRF astrometric references at 2026-10-20 02:00 and 02:01 UTC. The original API replies and full request URLs are retained. Other dates return `unsupported` with an incomplete mask; no ephemeris interpolation is implied. At these sample instants the observer response includes body directions and approximate geocentric horizon coordinates, with lunar topocentric parallax explicitly omitted. Reference replay is checked to 1e-9 degrees; this tolerance describes reproducing stored numerical fields, not the accuracy of the approximate observer model.

The index partitions RA/declination into 5-degree cells and filters candidate cells by exact spherical-cap geometry. It handles RA wrap and caps reaching either pole. Benchmarks cover actual 10,000-, 100,000- and 119,625-row HYG sets. A separately labeled one-million-row stress test repeats catalog rows and establishes capacity only. It does not establish a million distinct measured sources. Gaia DR3 ingest remains open.

`discover(seed=42, null_trials=99, max_mag=3.8)` extracts a four-star nearest-neighbor motif from raw vectors and magnitudes. The miner receives no names, constellation membership or templates. It freezes a source-ID/score/algorithm hash before historical data are loaded. Salience rewards brightness, compactness and noncollapsed separation ratios. Independent training and heldout seeds randomize RA while preserving catalog declinations and magnitudes; every null trial reruns the entire maximum-score search. A plus-one empirical heldout rank therefore includes the search selection effect. Only one winning motif is assessed. The conditional null is limited: it does not capture the full Milky Way's spatial correlations.

After mining and calibration, the benchmark loads pinned Stellarium modern skyculture line-art data (CC BY-SA 4.0, Stellarium team; original description/license retained). The winner contains Ginan, Acrux, Gacrux and Mimosa. Its source vertices overlap three of the four Southern Cross template vertices and recover one of its two traditional edges. Its constructed complete graph is not the traditional line drawing. The heldout result is recorded honestly as a rarity test, including a negative or inconclusive decision when the empirical p-value exceeds the threshold. The complete geometric miner is rerun at both guarded epoch endpoints (1900 and 2100); the same winner is selected, and edge drift is reported. No astrology, causal sky/road connection or Gaia-scale discovery is claimed.

Evidence is in `evidence/sky_source_verification.json`, `sky_observer_tampa.json`, `sky_discovery.json`, `sky_numerical.json`, and `sky_index_benchmark.json`. Run `python -m pytest tests/test_sky.py -q` for numerical, source, horizon, epoch guard, spherical-index, independent-discovery and reference-replay checks.
